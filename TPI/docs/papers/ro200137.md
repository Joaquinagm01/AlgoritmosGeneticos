## A HYBRID GENETIC ALGORITHM FOR STOCHASTIC JOB-SHOP SCHEDULING PROBLEMS

## Mohammed Boukedroun1,* , David Duvivier2,* , Abdessamad Ait-el-Cadi3,2 , Vincent Poirriez2 and Moncef Abbas1 [URL 🔗](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-6382-6588)

Abstract. Job-shop scheduling problems are among most studied problems in last years because of their importance for industries and manufacturing processes. They are classified as NP-hard problems in the strong sense. In order to tackle these problems several models and methods have been used. In this paper, we propose a hybrid metaheuristic composed of a genetic algorithm and a tabu search algorithm to solve the stochastic job-shop scheduling problem. Our contribution is based on a study of the perturbations that affect the processing times of the jobs. These perturbations, due to machine failures, occur according to a Poisson process; the results of our approach are validated on a set of instances originating from the OR-Library (Beasley, J. Oper. Res. Soc. 41 (1990) 1069–1072). On the basis of these instances, the hybrid metaheuristic is used to solve the stochastic job-shop scheduling problem with the objective of minimizing the makespan as first objective and the number of critical operations as second objective during the robustness analysis. Indeed, the results show that a high value of the number of critical operations is linked to high variations of the makespan of the perturbed schedules, or in other words to a weak robustness of the relating GA’s best schedule. Consequently, critical operations are not only good targets for optimizing a schedule, but also a clue of its goodness when considering stochastic and robustness aspects: the less critical operations it contains, the better it is.

## 2. Target scheduling problems

In a first part of this section, we focus on the usual definition of the deterministic JSP. ## 2.1. Definition of the standard deterministic JSP

There are various definitions and variants of the JSP, some of them include additional material/human resource constraints (see for instance [15, 40, 129]). In this paper, the JSP definition is closely related to the version known as the “standard” deterministic JSP [23, 52, 70, 73]. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

Definition 1 (JSP 𝑛 × 𝑚). Given a set 𝒥 = {𝐽𝑖} of 𝑛 jobs and a set ℳ of 𝑚 machines. Each job 𝐽𝑖 is composed of a set of 𝑚 operations {𝑂𝑖𝑗} among which there are precedence constraints, resulting in a total order relation ≺ between operations 𝑂𝑖𝑗 of a given job 𝐽𝑖. Each operation 𝑂𝑖𝑗 must be performed on only one machine 𝑀𝑗 during 𝑝𝑖𝑗 units of time (𝑝𝑖𝑗 > 0). Consequently, each job has one operation that has to be performed on each machine.

The goal is to minimize the makespan (denoted by 𝐶max), which is the total time elapsed from the beginning of the execution of the first operation that is scheduled to the end of execution of the last scheduled operation. Moreover, each job is supposed to be available at time 𝑡 = 0 and due-dates are not considered.


The deterministic version of the JSP could be formalized, using the three-field notation of scheduling problems proposed by Graham et al. [53], as: [URL 🔗](#page-0)

The following conditions must be fulfilled:

- (1) All jobs have to be performed.

- (2) A job is accomplished by processing the operations it is composed of.

- (3) There is only one machine that is able to perform any operation.

- (4) The operations must be performed in a predefined order. Hence, the operations of a job cannot be executed concurrently on different machines.

- (5) A machine can only perform one operation at a time.

- (6) An operation cannot be interrupted (i.e. no preemption).

Let us now define a few notions that are used in the rest of the paper. First of all, the result of the optimization of a JSP instance is a schedule denoted by 𝑠. It is a structure that indicates at any time, for each machine, the operation that is currently performed, unless the machine is idle. The number of jobs and machines of a given schedule 𝑠 are respectively denoted by 𝑛(𝑠) and 𝑚(𝑠). As in [20], semi-active and active schedules are defined as follows. A schedule is called semi-active if no job (operation) can be processed earlier without changing the processing order or violating the constraints. A schedule is called active if it is not possible to schedule jobs (operations) earlier without violating some constraints. As a consequence, an active schedule is also semi-active. The reverse is not true. It is known that an optimal schedule is active [46]. Indeed, an active schedule is such that no operation may be started earlier resulting in a reduction of the overall makespan (without violating some constraints). In an informal way, given a schedule 𝑠, a critical path is a sequence of operations that cannot start earlier than their current starting date due to precedence constraints and/or machine activity and such that any delay on one or several operation(s) on a critical path leads to increase the makespan of 𝑠. A schedule might comprise one or several critical path(s). A critical operation is an operation on a critical path. Hence, critical operations are good targets for local optimization of a schedule. Consequently, our metaheuristic focuses on critical operations and consider prioritary active and/or semi-active schedules. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

Even if initial famous deterministic JSP instances have been defined in the 60s, there are still many new models and approaches [11,13,69,71,97,100,101,116,124,136]. Next section is dedicated to one extension of the JSP, i.e. the stochastic JSP. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## 2.2. Definition of the stochastic JSP

The stochastic job-shop scheduling problem (SJSP) is an extension ofthe JSP [28,37,99,132,133]. As for the JSP, there are many models, approaches and variants for the SJSP. Mainly, the difference with the standard JSP is that some ofthe problem parameters are stochastic. The uncertainties in a scheduling problem could have many sources. The most common example of uncertainty studied in the SJSP is the randomness in processing times of operations; this is legitimated by the fact that processing times are not known in advance. But in some manufacturing systems, where the production process is mastered, the processing time of operations is deterministic and the stochasticity, often, comes from the machines. Thus, in the present work, this is exactly whatwe are interested in. In order to solve a JSP in stochastic environment, it is assumed that the probability distribution of the random variables is known in advance. The realized values of these random parameters can only be known at the end of the processing of the jobs, which makes the problem difficult and challenging. So the solution should ideally be robust enough such that whatever was the realized values, the quality of the solution holds. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

In the literature, many researchers consider the processing times being random and generated on the basis ofnor- mal, exponential or uniform distributions [21]. They use mainly two approaches to solve the SJSP with stochastic processing times: classical methods and simulation based methods [117, 128]. To compensate for the stochasticity, classical methods replace stochastic parameters by deterministic values, associated with parameters’ distributions. In general, stochastic data are replaced, either by some percentile of the distribution, or by the expected value mul- tiplied by a robustness factor. The quality of the solutions produced by these classical methods depends on the [URL 🔗](#page-0)


correct amount of slack included in the chosen deterministic values. Simulation based methods, combine heuristics and simulation, as for instance in [7,45,117]. In the objective function, the comparison between the current solution and a neighbor is based on simulating the solutions several times (so-called replicates). [URL 🔗](#page-0)

In this paper, the stochasticity comes from the environment, the shop-floor itself. The processing times of operations are deterministic but the execution of the jobs on the machines may experience some troubles. These perturbations are frequently induced by some minimal failures and lead to random effective processing times. The version of the SJSP that is used in this paper is defined in the following way:

Definition 2 (SJSP 𝑛 × 𝑚). Given a set 𝒥 = {𝐽𝑖} of 𝑛 jobs and a set ℳ of 𝑚 machines. Each job 𝐽𝑖 is composed of a set of 𝑚 operations {𝑂𝑖𝑗} among which there are precedence constraints, resulting in a total order relation ≺ between operations 𝑂𝑖𝑗 of a given job 𝐽𝑖. Each operation 𝑂𝑖𝑗 must be performed on only one machine 𝑀𝑗 and has a deterministic processing time 𝑝𝑖𝑗 but, due to the possible breakdowns on 𝑀𝑗 , the effective processing will take 𝑝+ 𝑖𝑗 units of time. Each effective processing time (𝑝+ 𝑖𝑗 > 0) is stochastic. As for the standard JSP, each job has one operation that has to be performed on each machine.

The motivation behind Definition 2 is that in real life, many manufacturing systems comprise machines that repetitively perform the same tasks. In such case, the processing times are deterministic and the randomness arises from breakdowns and repairs. It may also emerge as a result of other aspects like the workload allocation but not only from the tasks. The definition of the effective version of SJSP that is used in the presented work considers the case of randomness coming from machine failures. Moreover, as for JSP, each job is supposed to be available at time 𝑡 = 0 and due-dates are not considered. Consequently, the only “source of uncertainty” lies in the effective processing times 𝑝+ 𝑖𝑗 which are estimated on the basis of historical data about breakdowns. To be more precise, the considered SJSP is described as a mathematical model in Section 2.4. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## 2.3. Stochastic effects of unreliable machines on job durations

The purpose of this section is to present the stochastic effect of unreliable machines on job durations and to compute the distribution of the effective processing time of jobs’ operations.

In a stochastic environment where random events may negatively affect the productivity rate of a machine, job durations become chaotic. Therefore, the scheduling process should be robust enough to handle these per- turbations. Thus, the impact of these random events can be absorbed to a certain extent and their consequences


limited (the interested reader may find additional information in [18]). In this paper, preventive maintenance is assumed to be performed “outside” production periods (during weekends for instance). However, these pre- ventive maintenance actions are not sufficient to avoid failures. Consequently, the effective production time is increased due to the failures themselves and their repairs. The effect is stochastic and its impact on the job execution is translated by a non-deterministic duration. In the considered SJSP, after a repair, any machine is supposed to be as good as new. Before generalizing on all machines and jobs, the following paragraphs illus- trate the underlying computations by focusing on an unreliable machine and a single operation of known and deterministic duration 𝑝𝑖𝑗 . In the SJSP, any operation on any machine is prone to be interrupted one or several time(s) due to failure(s) or perturbation(s) as illustrated in Figure 1. However, when no failure occurs, the operation is never interrupted as in the standard JSP. If a failure or perturbation occurs, there is no need to restart the current operation, i.e. the operation is resumed to finish its processing. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## 3. Algorithms for Phase1 and Phase2

Among several tested encodings, better results have been obtained with direct encoding (the data structure actually represents a schedule). Consequently, we use a two-dimensional direct encoding composed, for each machine, of the list of operations that are performed with their time slot. This encoding makes it easier to detect available time slots as well as critical operations of a schedule. The major drawback of such an encoding is the complexity of operators that have to be designed to maintain validity of solutions. In this paper we focus on a hybrid algorithm based on a TS and a GA, so called HGA.

Let us now turn to a brief presentation of TS & GA metaheuristics.

## 3.1. Tabu search

Among the Stochastic Local Search (SLS) algorithms [67], Tabu Search (TS) [48, 49] is a SLS which is able to escape from local optima thanks to a mechanism that avoids “cycling around” local optima. One delicate part in using a TS is the choice of the local move operator. Using our direct encoding, the basic move is an exchange (swap) of two operations (focusing on critical operations) on a randomly chosen machine. No intensification/diversification mechanism is implemented, however the size of the Tabu-list varies dynamically during the run according to [63]: a short list favors intensification, while a long list improves the diversification mechanism. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## 3.2. Genetic algorithm

Genetic Algorithms (GAs) are stochastic search methods inspired by the biological evolution of living beings [50,64,65,89]. In this paper, starting from a standard generational GA [93], the operators, the objective function and the scheme of application of the operators have been tailored to solve the JSP. The recombination operator is based on the GA/GT crossover [90,131], which relies on Giffler and Thompson’s algorithm [46]. The mutation, so-called invert8, swaps two operations (focusing on critical operations) on a randomly chosen machine, and re-schedules operations as soon as possible in the schedule. ## 4. Robustness measure and assessment

Except for special cases where uncertainties are negligible (in comparison to average processing times for example) the HGA should be able to deal with problems of a stochastic nature in an efficient way. A solution that is relatively insensitive with respect to changes in the problem data it is based on, is called robust. In our


present work, we deal with the quality robustness, the robustness of a schedule. It indicates that its quality (i.e. the value of the objective function) does not deviate much from the optimal value when the problem data changes (i.e. over a set of instances, or in other words, in a changing environment). The quality robustness matters before the changes occur in the problem data. The aim of next sub-section is to define this robustness.

## 4.1. Robustness definition

Several definitions of robust scheduling exist, such as: robust scheduling is a scheduling whose performances are acceptable in the presence ofuncertainties, or from [9]: a scheduling is robust if its performance is not sensitive to the uncertainty ofthe data. Our study, devoted to SJSP, focuses on the perturbation of the processing times of the instances of the problem. For each instance 𝜎 of the (deterministic) JSP, the goal is to generate a set {best𝑟}𝜎 of efficient schedules ofgood performance on the basis ofthe makespan and – in a second step – evaluate the robustness of each schedule best𝑟𝜎 ∈ {best𝑟}𝜎 via a set of 19 perturbed SJSP’s schedules. Consequently, the robustness of the generated schedules is evaluated on the basis of the variation of the makespan when processing times 𝑝𝑖𝑗 vary (according to Eq. (3)). [URL 🔗](#page-0)

Beyond the measurement (a posteriori) of the variations of the makespan, we do not control anything (a priori) because we are subject to temporal variations (machine unavailability), so we can only act before the occurrence of disturbances on the number of critical operations (𝑁cop) present in the proposed schedules sent to production. Indeed, the higher 𝑁cop, the more sensitive the schedule is likely to be when any changes occur in the processing time(s) of one or more operation(s). Consequently, a schedule with less critical operations should be more robust. This is illustrated by Figure A.1, where critical operations as well as conjunctive and disjunctive constraints implied in the critical paths are highlighted. Without modifying the order of operations, each increase of the processing time of any critical operation leads to an increase of the makespan. It is supposed that dynamic rescheduling of operations is not allowed since this is not allowed in standard JSP, i.e. only left or right shifts of operations3 are allowed. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

## 6. Discussion and perspectives

In this study, a GA is hybridized with a TS and the GT algorithm to generate robust and efficient schedules as solutions of the SJSP problem. Our algorithm is efficient and provides us with very good solutions on the sets of JSP and SJSP instances. The hybrid GA is based on a parallel scheme of application of the operators and parameterized to avoid premature convergence.

In this paper, the hybrid metaheuristic is used to solve the SJSP with the objective of minimizing the makespan as first objective and the number of critical operations as second objective during the robustness analysis. Indeed, for the standard JSP, aiming at optimizing the makespan, the usual idea is to design the objective function as returning the makespan of a schedule. However, critical operations are good targets for local optimization of a schedule since they have a direct impact on the value of the makespan. On top of this, when considering stochastic and robustness aspects, the number of critical operations may be used as a clue of the goodness of a schedule: the less critical operations it contains, the better it is. Indeed, the more critical operations in the best JSP schedule generated by the hybrid GA, the greater the likelihood that the temporal disruption of a processing time associated with an operation will have an impact on the value of the makespan of this schedule. This is illustrated by the presented results that show variations in the makespan of perturbed SJSP’s schedules (increase related to machine unavailability). Unfortunately, nothing is being controlled because processing times are subject to temporal variations, so we can only act before the occurrence of disturbances on the number of critical operations present in the proposed schedules sent to production. Consequently, through the critical operations density, the number of critical operations has been considered in the robustness analysis as a way to guide the “a priori choice” of a schedule. To support the results, thanks to the computation of several correlation coefficients, we compare robust and deterministic solutions on the basis of the observed makespan distribution in order to assess the impact of random perturbations on the makespan, depending on the number of critical operations. The presented results show that a high value of the number of critical operations is linked to high variations of the makespan of the perturbed schedules, or in other words to a weak robustness of the relating GA’s best schedule.

Hence, in future works it seems interesting to use an objective function which combines the makespan and the number of critical operations. The idea of using lexicographical and multicriteria approaches is not new in the literature [4, 7, 17, 35, 36, 38, 42, 47, 61, 66, 72, 87, 92, 96, 105, 125, 129], however on the basis of pre- sented results we argue that using a lexicographical sort is possible and sufficient to distinguish schedules that have the same value of makespan, from the point of view of the number of critical operations whereas the makespan remains the most important criterion, as presented in [32, 33]. In the next version of our HGA, even the quality of the solutions of the JSP will be measured by this lexicographical sort during Phase1 and Phase2. This has not been implemented in the presented HGA, since this might slow down its convergence as mentioned in [32]. Other criteria should also be considered in the objective function. Indeed, as exam- ples of such criteria, recent international developments have strengthened current research on the inclusion of energy problems and their relevant effects on the scheduling costs, leading to green manufacturing aspects [7, 25, 38, 42, 96, 105, 129, 130]. [URL 🔗](#page-0)


Moreover, we are also working on the profiling and optimization of the HGA’s underlying C-code so as to tackle JSP’s instances of larger sizes. Particular attention will be paid on the instances composing the Set IV, for which the hybrid GA has not reached sufficiently good solutions.
